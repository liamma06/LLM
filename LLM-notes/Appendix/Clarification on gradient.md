The partial derivative is the individual slope for one single weight, answering the question "if I tweak just this weight, how much does the loss change?" Each weight's partial derivative is calculated independently using the chain rule, meaning it is not derived from or subtracted from any other weight's calculation. The chain rule multiplies together the slopes of every step between that weight and the loss, so w1 which is furthest back multiplies through all three steps, while w2 one step closer only multiplies through two steps. The difference in chain length is simply because there are fewer steps between that weight and the loss, not because anything was removed or subtracted. The gradient is simply the collection of all those independently calculated partial derivatives bundled together into one vector, where each value is mapped to its specific weight. Backpropagation is the process that actually performs all those independent chain rule calculations by rewinding through the computation graph from the loss backwards. Once backpropagation finishes, each weight has its own personal gradient value, and the optimizer uses that individual value to adjust that specific weight, not the gradient as a whole, but each weight's own partial derivative pulled out of it, plugged into the formula: new weight = old weight minus (gradient x learning rate).
